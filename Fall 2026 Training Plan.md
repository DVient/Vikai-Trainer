EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROGRAM OBJECTIVES
This training system is designed to transform an unknown, slender point guard into a high-impact provincial prospect while managing extreme logistical and fatigue constraints.
1. Primary Physical Objectives
●	Strength & Mass Development: Increase functional lean muscle mass (target: 150–155 lbs over 6 months) to improve mass-to-height ratio (6'0" / 140\text{ lbs}) and structural force absorption without compromising speed or joint integrity.
●	Explosiveness & Force Production: Increase kinetic impulse (F = ma) via high-concentric strength work, low-impact plyometrics, and full-recovery sprint acceleration.
●	Multi-Directional Change of Direction (COD): Master sharp 90-degree plant-and-cut mechanics, 5-10-5 shuttle footwork, and deceleration braking without wasting false steps.
2. Primary Basketball Skill Objectives
●	Space Creation: Master 1-dribble punch-drags, step-backs, and side-steps into fluid mid-range pull-up jumpers.
●	Two-Foot Paint Finishes: Establish two-foot stride stops and jump stops in traffic to lower center of mass, absorb contact against 6'4"+ rim protectors, and elevate vertically.
●	Visual Scanning & IQ: Develop continuous head-up scanning during ball-handling and pick-and-roll drives to instantly read primary coverage (drop vs. hedge) and secondary helpers (taggers).
3. Charlie Francis System Integration
●	High/Low CNS Split: Consolidate all maximal neural stressors (sprinting, lifting, heavy skill work, team practice) onto High CNS days. Keep Low CNS days strictly under 70% intensity to allow full central nervous system restoration.
●	Vertical Integration: Train speed, strength, work capacity, and skills concurrently year-round, adjusting volume rather than removing qualities.
●	Extensive Tempo: Utilize low-intensity grass running (60\text{--}65\% speed) on recovery days to increase capillary density, flush metabolic waste, and preserve joint structures.
CRITICAL CRITIQUE: COACHING CONDITIONING REALITY VS. ATHLETE DEVELOPMENT
An "old-school" practice structure featuring 6 sets of 17s, 6 sets of 2-minute wall squats, continuous wall touches, and defensive slide 17s represents an extreme glycolytic and eccentric stress load.
Coach's Conditioning Load	Athlete Biomechanical Reality	Programmatic Risk
6x 17s (High Lactic Acidosis)	140\text{ lbs} at 6'0" (Low energy reserve)	Neural motor pattern degradation & weight loss
6x 2-min Wall Squats (Quad Isometric)	Growth plate & tendon traction stress	Severe patellar tendinopathy (Jumper's Knee)
2-min Wall Touches (Continuous Impact)	Light frame / vulnerable tendon insertions	Joint inflammation & Achilles/patellar strain
Defensive Slide 17s (Lateral Glycolytic)	High lateral strain on groin/adductors	Adductor strain & hip flexor fatigue
Strategic Program Modifications
1.	Categorization: Tuesday and Thursday practice nights are classified as Maximal Glycolytic & Muscular Fatigue Days.
2.	Gym Adjustment: Zero heavy lower-body lifting is performed on practice days. Pre-practice gym work on Tuesday/Thursday is strictly limited to upper-body strength, core anti-rotation, and neural priming.
3.	Primary Strength Engine Shift: Heavy squatting, deadlifting, and maximum velocity sprinting are moved to Saturday morning, when the athlete is 36 hours clear of the coach’s conditioning load.
4.	Active Joint Preservation: Wednesdays and Fridays incorporate targeted isometric holds (Spanish squats, Copenhagen planks) to inhibit patellar pain receptors and reinforce adductor strength.
